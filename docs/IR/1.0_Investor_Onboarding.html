01 What & Why

What was built. A dedicated onboarding funnel for prospective investors. A Lead created with the Investor Onboarding record type is automatically routed to a queue, the IR team is notified, a team member claims it, reviews it, and converts it — and the conversion produces exactly one thing: an investor Contact (record type Investor) with no Account and no Opportunity attached. The new investor automatically receives a branded welcome email.

Why it exists. DPEG runs two very different populations through the same standard objects: acquisition brokers and investors. The Acquisitions side has its own heavy conversion cascade (deal trees, Property records, Broker record-type stamping). Investors must never fall into that machinery, and the business explicitly does not want investor Contacts parented to Accounts or generating Opportunities. This branch builds the IR lane and the guardrails that keep the two lanes apart.

The one-sentence requirement

When a Lead with record type Investor Onboarding is converted, create only a Contact — if the platform forces an Account into existence to make conversion legal, create it temporarily, detach it from the Contact, and delete it permanently, all in the same transaction.

02 The Lead Lifecycle

The Status path is enforced by a dedicated Business Process and surfaced as a Path on the record page. Each transition is driven by a quick action that only appears when it is the correct next step (Dynamic Actions, section 06).

New
Created via web, email-to-lead or manual entry. A before-save flow assigns the Lead to the Investor Onboarding Queue; an after-save flow emails every active queue member.
→
New (claimed)
Assign to Me — a queue member takes ownership. Non-members are refused with an error screen.
→
Under Review
Under Review quick action stamps the status while the IR rep vets the prospect.
→
Converted
Create Profile validates, then converts via Apex: investor Contact created, temp Account destroyed, no Opportunity. Welcome email sent.

Statuses for this record type: New → Under Review → Create Profile → Converted (LeadStatus standard value set extended; only Converted is flagged as a converted status).

05 The Conversion Engine (Apex)

Two new classes do the work; one existing class was modified to stay out of the way.

ClassLayerRole

InvestorLeadConvertSelector NEWSelector All SOQL for the feature. Lead read runs WITH USER_MODE (user-initiated); the cascade-delete safety re-query runs WITH SYSTEM_MODE so an FLS gap cannot disable the cleanup org-wide.
LeadConvertService MODIFIEDService The Acquisitions conversion cascade now explicitly skips any Lead on the Investor_Onboarding record type. Without this guard it would stamp the investor Contact as Broker and build Property records — a silent visibility change, since sharing rules key on record type.

How a conversion runs — seven steps, one transaction

  1. Insert a temporary Account named IR Conversion Temp Account <leadId>, record type Investor_Entity (describe-resolved, never a hardcoded Id). Why: Lead.Company is optional and Person Accounts are enabled — left alone, the platform would mint a Person Account. Naming it after the Lead makes any orphan self-identifying.
  2. Convert via Database.convertLead with the temp Account, setDoNotCreateOpportunity(true), status Converted — and deliberately no setContactId. Why: pre-creating a Contact always tripped DUPLICATES_DETECTED, because the org’s Contact duplicate rule matches new Contacts against open Leads — i.e. the very Lead being converted. Letting the conversion mint the Contact sidesteps the rule entirely.
  3. Read the new Contact Id from the conversion result. Name, Email and Phone are mapped by the platform itself — Apex never copies standard fields.
  4. Detach — one update writing AccountId = null and nothing else. Why one field: any extra field is another way for this safety write to fail. A previous combined write failed on an unrelated field, stranded the temp Account, and a later manual cleanup cascade-deleted two real investor Contacts. This design is the post-mortem fix.
  5. Confirm from the database — re-query the Contacts and only trust a stored AccountId = null. Why: a SaveResult proves a statement was accepted, not that a field holds a value. Deleting an Account cascade-deletes its Contacts, so the only safe precondition is a re-read. Fails closed: an unreadable Contact strands the Account instead of risking the Contact.
  6. Delete the temp Account — permanently. Database.delete followed by Database.emptyRecycleBin, the purge scoped to Investor_Onboarding leads only. Why hard delete: the requirement is “removed from the org, not restorable.” Measured: same-transaction emptyRecycleBin works — a subsequent undelete fails with UNDELETE_FAILED, while the soft-delete control arm restores fine. The row may still read IsDeleted = true under ALL ROWS until the background purge; that is retention, not failure.
  7. Enrich — stamp Contact record type Investor and carry Is_Accredited__c + Referred_By__c from the Lead. Why last: a failure here costs two field values an admin can set by hand; nothing is at risk because the Contact is already detached and the Account already gone.
Governor shape is a contract

2 SOQL / at most 5 DML + 1 convertLead call — constant regardless of how many Leads are converted. The safety re-query (SOQL 2) and the standalone detach (DML 2) were deliberate widenings of an older, cheaper, unsafe shape; the class header forbids “optimising” them back out.

After conversion — read the right field

The Lead’s ConvertedAccountId points at the deleted temporary Account — do not navigate to it. The durable link is ConvertedContactId. ConvertedOpportunityId is null by construction.

The FLS lesson this branch paid for

Contact.AccountId read/edit was missing from every parent the IR persona held — and it is the one field the detach step writes. The symptom: record type and Is Accredited saved (step 7) while the Contact stayed attached to its temp Account (step 4 failed), for IR users only, while admins converted cleanly. The grant now lives explicitly in DPEG_IR_Default_Access with a comment marking it load-bearing.

08 Email Templates

Two Lightning (SFX) templates in the IR_Email_Templates_Lightning folder:

  • IR Lead Notification Email — sent to each queue member when a new IR lead arrives. Deliberately carries no Lead merge fields (measured platform limit when the recipient is a User).
  • Investor Welcome Email (Investor_Welcome_Email_LTN) — branded, table-based, inline-styled HTML welcome sent to the new investor Contact. The _LTN suffix exists because EmailTemplate DeveloperName is unique org-wide and a classic template already owned the natural name.
Templates are records, not metadata — and the Builder can destroy them

The Metadata API cannot carry HtmlValue as Lightning Builder content, so both templates are loaded as data by scripts/load-lightning-email-templates.apex (idempotent upsert by DeveloperName; body embedded as base64; script kept under the ~32,000-character anonymous Apex ceiling). Source of truth is the paired .html/.json files in scripts/email-templates/. Never edit these templates in Email Template Builder — saving there regenerates HtmlValue from the canvas and wipes the designed body (this happened once and reduced the welcome email to a single plain-text line). To change a template: edit the HTML file, regenerate the blob, re-run the loader.

09 Testing

The suite was rewritten against the current design (the original tests asserted the pre-redesign “convert onto an existing Contact” premise and failed against correct code). What the suite pins down now:

  • The four invariants, each with its own falsifier: a Contact is created; it ends with no Account; the temp Account is permanently gone; no Opportunity exists.
  • Hard delete is asserted by undeletability — Database.undelete must fail — never by IsDeleted, which stays true under ALL ROWS until the background purge. A non-IR control arm proves the test can tell hard from soft.
  • Refusals: null/empty input, non-Lead Id, already converted — all typed messages, index-parallel results in mixed batches.
  • Governor shape at bulk: 50 leads convert with the contracted 2 SOQL / ≤5 DML. 50, not the project-standard 251, because Database.convertLead caps at 100 per transaction — a 251 test would fail on the platform limit before proving anything. The constant-cost assertion is strictly stronger than a volume test.
  • New factory method TestDataFactory.createInvestorOnboardingLeads: builds IR-record-type leads that satisfy the Create Profile gates, then hands ownership back to the running user — because the assignment flow queues new leads and convertLead refuses queue-owned ones (measured error documented in the method).
  • InvestorLeadConvertSelectorTest covers the selector directly, including the empty-input SOQL short-circuits (asserted via Limits.getQueries(), not just empty results) and the attached-vs-detached readings of the safety re-query.
